04

What happens when you visit a link

The server needs to process the request to find the alias, apply rules and return the redirect. It may read the IP address, user-agent, language, referrer, campaign and technical headers. The application database does not store a visitor's IP address in plain text for identification: it generates a daily HMAC hashof the IP address and user-agent to count distinct pseudonyms within each day. The same person may be counted again on another day, and different devices or networks may produce different values.

An important distinction.The IP address may be processed temporarily by the operating system, proxy, network, hosting, security logs and hCaptcha. The applicable infrastructure and region are published in the recipients section.

Country information may come from a trusted infrastructure header where configured. UVH does not seek to obtain a precise location. Redirect rules can use country, language, device, operating system, time, referrer or campaign; their technical operation is explained in the automation section. The workspace must assess any use of these rules that significantly affects individuals.

A daily hash is a form of pseudonymisation, not a guarantee of irreversible anonymity. These events still require protection, access controls and a legal basis. Visitor counts refer to each day's pseudonyms and do not establish the number of unique people across several days.

The analytics queue receives reduced dimensions and a visitor hash; it does not include the full IP address or user-agent. This does not establish that a proxy, provider or system log does not process them. The referring domain is extracted from the referrer; a campaign parameter may also contain information entered by the workspace or the source of the traffic.

05

Anti-abuse verification with hCaptcha

When enabled, sign-in, registration and certain public recovery, resend and reporting flows integrate hCaptcha, a service provided by Intuition Machines, Inc. Its purpose is to distinguish human interaction from abusive automation, spam and fraud. When the widget is loaded or completed, hCaptcha may process the IP address, browser characteristics, interaction timing, movements and other technical signals.

UVH receives a token and verifies it with hCaptcha on the server; it does not receive the challenge solution or share your password. Abuse prevention pursues a legitimate security interest that must be assessed for the actual configuration, together with the information and safeguards applicable to the provider's processing. hCaptcha acts as a provider and may process data in the EEA and, where applicable, the United States.

Read the hCaptcha Privacy Policyand its terms. If the provider is unavailable, UVH fails securely and does not skip verification.

hCaptcha is used in functions where anti-abuse verification is enabled. Its processing is limited to protecting those flows and is separate from managing your UVH account.

hCaptcha's documentation distinguishes processing on the customer's instructions from processing for which the provider determines its own purposes. Read its policy for details of those roles and safeguards. Non-essential technologies requiring consent must remain disabled until consent is obtained; anti-abuse protection does not authorise additional advertising purposes.

06

Cookies and browser storage

UVH uses technologies strictly necessary for security, continuity and preferences:

  • Session cookie:identifies an authenticated session; it is HttpOnly and cannot be read by JavaScript.
  • CSRF cookie:enables double-submit protection for API operations.
  • Local storage:keeps the theme, selected workspace and update signals between tabs. Invitation and pending-intent secrets are not stored in localStorage: the current flow keeps them in HttpOnly cookies. An intent lasts 24 hours by default; an invitation lasts seven days. The cookie for editing a pending registration has its own default lifetime of 24 hours.
  • hCaptcha:may use strictly necessary storage according to its own documentation and configuration.

First-party authentication, continuity and security cookies serve requested functions. The necessity and purposes of each third-party technology must be assessed separately. Clearing them may sign you out, reset preferences or prevent recovery of a pending intent.

09

Retention and deletion

We apply the storage limitation principle. Retention periods depend on purpose and configuration:

  • Account and workspace:while active, and afterwards only for as long as necessary to meet obligations or handle claims.
  • Link intent:24 hours, unless completed or discarded sooner.
  • Sessions:until they expire or are revoked; expired records are purged by scheduled tasks.
  • Analytics:the current configuration provides a default operational retention period of 180 days, subject to the service configuration.
  • Audit records:the current configuration provides 365 days to trace sensitive actions.
  • Abuse reports and security evidence:for as long as necessary to investigate, take action, prevent duplicates and defend claims, followed by erasure or anonymisation.
  • Backups:until their secure rotation cycle is complete.

Deleting an account may not immediately erase data that must be retained to meet legal obligations, maintain security or defend claims. Retention exceptions must be limited to their purpose and to a justified period.

Deletion from operational systems and backup rotation are separate processes. The full backup cycle has yet to be confirmed; we do not promise a deadline for complete deletion from all copies that we have not been able to verify. Retained backups must have restricted access and must not be reused for routine purposes.

The default configuration also retains export files for seven days and privacy requests for 1,095 days. These periods are limited to delivering the export and maintaining a record of how rights requests were handled, respectively. Expired tokens, revoked sessions, mail jobs and logs have their own clean-up periods; these do not necessarily match the account retention period.

Where data must be blocked under applicable law, it is no longer available for ordinary use and is retained with restricted access for the relevant liabilities. Once the purpose or justified retention period ends, it must be effectively erased or anonymised. A backup does not authorise the resumption of a use that has already been discontinued.

Your rights

You may request access, rectification, erasure, objection, restriction, portability and protection from solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. You may also withdraw consent without affecting earlier processing.

Write to privacidad@uvh.eswith your request. We may ask for proportionate information to verify your identity without collecting more than necessary. Exercising your rights is free of charge, except for manifestly unfounded or excessive requests in the circumstances permitted by law.

We will generally respond within one month, with extensions where the law permits. If you are dissatisfied, you may complain to the Spanish Data Protection Agencyor the competent authority where you live.

The dashboard includes a privacy requests section for authenticated accounts, with progress tracking and messaging. Account exports and controls are also available. These tools help you exercise your rights, but visitors, invitees and people without access are not required to open an account or pass an MFA check they cannot complete.

Your request should identify the right you wish to exercise and provide a proportionate way to locate the data. Do not send a copy of an identity document as a matter of course; additional verification will be requested only where necessary and proportionate. If the workspace is the data controller, the request will be coordinated according to the applicable roles and instructions.

We will generally respond within one month. Any extension due to complexity or volume must be communicated within the first month, with reasons, and must not become an automatic delay for every request. If a request is refused or restricted, the reason and relevant complaint options must be explained.

11

Security measures

UVH applies measures proportionate to the risk: password hashing, HttpOnly sessions, CSRF protection, email verification, MFA, hCaptcha, workspace roles, scoped tokens, encryption of sensitive secrets, audit records, rate limits and SSRF protection for outbound requests.

No system is infallible. Protect your credentials, enable MFA when available, review sessions and tokens, and report incidents to soporte@uvh.es. If a security breach creates a risk that requires notification, we will act in accordance with applicable law.

Permissions are checked on the server, as well as through button visibility. Sensitive tokens and secrets must not appear unprotected in public responses, URLs or logs. Security features reduce risks, but do not in themselves demonstrate the effectiveness of incident management, training, hosting access controls or backups.

In the event of a security breach, the risk must be assessed, the breach contained, evidence preserved proportionately and any applicable notification obligations met. Informing affected people does not depend on whether they have an active account at the time. Where required, the supervisory authority and affected people will be notified under the conditions and within the time limits set by law.

Children

The Service is intended for people with the capacity to manage digital resources on their own behalf or for an organisation. It is not designed to deliberately collect children's data. If you believe a child has provided data without authorisation, write to us so that we can investigate and take appropriate action.

The age at which someone may consent to certain processing and their capacity to enter into a contract are separate requirements. In Spain, the fourteen-year threshold in the LOPDGDD concerns consent in the circumstances specified by that law; it does not in itself confer capacity to enter into any contract or remove other legal authorisation requirements.

Registration does not request a date of birth or carry out universal age verification. If a service aimed at children is introduced, or significant use by children is identified, the applicable information, legal bases, security and authorisation procedures must be adapted without collecting more data than necessary.

Rules and automated decisions

Link rules can select a destination based on country, language, device, operating system, time, referrer or campaign. These are technical routing decisions. Quotas, rate limits and security checks may stop a request or prevent an action that does not meet the requirements.

Using these rules does not authorise a workspace to make decisions with legal or similarly significant effects on people without meeting its own obligations. Targeting must not be used to discriminate unlawfully, conceal mandatory information or bypass controls.

UVH does not offer credit, employment or benefits eligibility decision features. If a feature with significant effects on people is introduced, its operation and relevant safeguards must be assessed and explained before use.
